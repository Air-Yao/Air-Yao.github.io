Abstract

Role-based multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) has been widely used to improve decentralized coordination. Existing role-based methods typically rely on finite role spaces that are predefined or induced from task-specific interactions, and represent roles as trainable embeddings optimized jointly with policies. Such roles are often difficult to interpret and tightly coupled with reward-driven training. This issue becomes critical under intermittent communication. Without reliable teammate information, implicit role embeddings fail to guide agents in maintaining complementary behaviors. To address this issue, we propose GeRo (Generative Role), a cooperative MARL framework driven by explicit semantic roles. Specifically, GeRo prompts a large language model (LLM) with task descriptions and observation semantics to generate task-conditioned roles with explicit behavioral meanings, alongside a heuristic role function. To prevent semantic degradation during reinforcement learning, these generated roles are processed through a frozen text encoder. At each timestep, GeRo dynamically selects roles by integrating the heuristic function with a learned evaluator, grounding the selection in local observations and short-horizon state-transition dynamics. By conditioning decentralized policies on these selected roles, GeRo establishes a robust semantic consensus, enabling agents to sustain coordination even under intermittent communication. Experiments on StarCraft Multi-Agent Challenge and Google Research Football show that GeRo outperforms baselines under intermittent communication, while further analyses verify role interpretability, role-action alignment, and robustness across different LLM generators. Overview of the proposed DIRECT framework. The top panel illustrates the recurrent information flow across timesteps, while the bottom panel details the computation at timestep $t$ under the Intention $\otimes$ Latent Rule formulation. Specifically, the Intention Forecaster predicts an intention representation $\mathbf{m}_t^i$ for each agent based on its recent action $a_{t-1}^i$. To handle communication volatility, the Latent Rule Inference Module maintains an episodic memory ($M^i_t, T^i_t$) and employs a time-decay gating mechanism to synthesize a latent rule $e_t^i$ from partial neighborhood signals. Finally, the Agent Policy Network conditions the local policy based on this latent rule-derived guidance, enabling decentralized coordination.
