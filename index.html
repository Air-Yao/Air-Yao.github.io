Abstract

Role-based multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) has been widely used to improve decentralized coordination. Existing role-based methods typically rely on finite role spaces that are predefined or induced from task-specific interactions, and represent roles as trainable embeddings optimized jointly with policies. Such roles are often difficult to interpret and tightly coupled with reward-driven training. This issue becomes critical under intermittent communication. Without reliable teammate information, implicit role embeddings fail to guide agents in maintaining complementary behaviors. To address this issue, we propose GeRo (Generative Role), a cooperative MARL framework driven by explicit semantic roles. Specifically, GeRo prompts a large language model (LLM) with task descriptions and observation semantics to generate task-conditioned roles with explicit behavioral meanings, alongside a heuristic role function. To prevent semantic degradation during reinforcement learning, these generated roles are processed through a frozen text encoder. At each timestep, GeRo dynamically selects roles by integrating the heuristic function with a learned evaluator, grounding the selection in local observations and short-horizon state-transition dynamics. By conditioning decentralized policies on these selected roles, GeRo establishes a robust semantic consensus, enabling agents to sustain coordination even under intermittent communication. Experiments on StarCraft Multi-Agent Challenge and Google Research Football show that GeRo outperforms baselines under intermittent communication, while further analyses verify role interpretability, role-action alignment, and robustness across different LLM generators. Although supporting coordination, they lack explicit behavioral semantics and are prone to entanglement with policy optimization, inherently limiting interpretability. Right: Empirical results on Google Research Football show that task-specific roles outperform default roles and strong baselines, while the role-wise action composition illustrates the behavioral alignment between generated semantic roles and executed action patterns.

Overall architecture of GeRo. Given the task description and semantic observation description, GeRo uses an LLM to generate task-conditioned semantic roles and a heuristic role function. The textual roles are encoded into fixed embeddings by a frozen semantic encoder, forming an interpretable role space separated from reinforcement learning optimization. At each timestep $t$, the heuristic function produces semantic role scores $u_i^t$, while the dynamic role evaluator estimates the role utility $v_i^t$ from the agent's local decision context and state-transition dynamics. The two signals are combined to select a suitable role $\rho_i^t$, and the embedding of the selected role conditions the downstream module for decentralized action selection. During training, the dynamic role evaluator is optimized by latent transition prediction and bootstrapped role-value prediction. During execution, the learned evaluator and role-conditioned policy operate over the fixed LLM-generated role space.
